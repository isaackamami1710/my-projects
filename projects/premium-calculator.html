Health Insurance Premium Rating Calculator

A working quotation model for two benefit categories, built in Excel on rate tables — and a locked edition that lets colleagues use it without exposing the pricing logic.

The problem

Medical quotations are repetitive and easy to get wrong by hand. A family moves from M+3 to M+4, the principal crosses an age band, the inpatient limit changes — and each of those silently changes the premium. Building the logic once, in a model that can be checked, removes arithmetic risk from the quoting conversation and makes the numbers defensible when a broker asks how they were derived.

What it does

  • Prices two benefit categories side by side, each with its own benefit limits.
  • Builds inpatient premium by family size — M, M+1, M+2 … through M+7 — from principal, spouse and child rates.
  • Adds outpatient (single member through M+5), dental, optical and maternity.
  • Applies age bands automatically: the age entered drives a lookup, so no manual band selection.
  • Adds levies and stamp duty, then rolls everything into a grand total.
  • Validates inputs with drop-down lists so a limit or age outside the product cannot be keyed in.

How the pricing is structured

Family size is priced by composition rather than by a single multiplier: M is the principal alone, M+1 adds the spouse, and each further step adds one child at the child rate. That mirrors how the premium actually accumulates, so a change in family structure is traceable line by line.

The locked edition

A calculator that exposes its formulas is a calculator somebody will edit. The second edition keeps the same visible output but protects the engine:

  • Only input cells are unlocked; every formula cell is locked and hidden, so it displays its value but not its logic.
  • Rate and support sheets are hidden at the deepest level the format allows and cannot be unhidden without the password.
  • Workbook structure is protected, so sheets cannot be added, revealed or reordered.
  • Row formatting and row insertion remain allowed, so users can still hide or edit rows in the working area.
Why two editions? The open edition is for building and auditing the model. The locked edition is for handing it to people who need the answer, not the method — the same discipline used when a rating model is shared outside the team that maintains it.

Verification approach

Every rate was checked against the source tables, and the workbook was recalculated end to end and compared with an independent recomputation: totals, all monthly rows and every percentage had to agree cell for cell, with zero error values anywhere in the file. Styling was diffed against the original sheet so the output view did not shift.

Rate tables and figures shown here are synthetic. No client, member, policy or employer-confidential information is included.
