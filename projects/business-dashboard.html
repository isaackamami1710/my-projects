New Business & Renewal Performance Dashboard

Ten months of a synthetic medical book, turned into one screen that answers the questions a production meeting actually asks — without anyone rebuilding a pivot table.

The problem

Underwriting and renewal reporting is usually assembled by hand each month: export, pivot, paste, format. It is slow, it breaks when someone inserts a row, and two people doing it produce two different answers. A dashboard built on live formulas over a single clean table removes that whole cycle — the source data changes, the view updates.

What it shows

Premium written10 months
Transactions350 policies

Business typesNew · Renewal · Transfer
  • Monthly trend — premium, policies and lives by month, plus average premium per policy and share of the period.
  • Business type mix — how much of the book is new, renewal or transferred.
  • Customer segment — retail, SME, corporate and city business.
  • Distribution channel — agency, broker, direct and branch.
  • Payment mode — bank transfer, M-Pesa, EFT and cheque.
  • Agency and client concentration — rank order, so dependency on a single producer is visible.

Design decisions

  • No pasted numbers. Every tile is a formula over the transaction table, so the dashboard cannot drift out of step with the data.
  • A separate support sheet holds the aggregation, keeping the visible dashboard uncluttered and auditable.
  • Part-months are labelled. A month that covers only ten days is marked as such, because a short month read against full months is a false signal.
  • Native charts, no embedded objects — the file stays portable and opens cleanly anywhere.
  • One print area, landscape A4 — the whole view fits a single page for a board pack.

The transferable idea: the same structure works for real production data. Swap the synthetic table for an export, keep the aggregation sheet, and the view rebuilds itself.

Verification approach

The workbook was recalculated end to end and every derived figure — totals, all ten monthly rows and every percentage — was re-derived independently from the raw transaction rows and compared cell for cell. The check ran to zero error values across every sheet.

The dataset in this project is synthetic: fictional clients, policy numbers and transactions. No real client, member or employer data is used or published.
